Candidate mass on the quantity the paper actually plots
=======================================================

Figure 3 of arXiv:2309.05728 plots the AVERAGE of the two candidate masses,
<m>_ABC = mean(m_0, m_1), not the two candidates separately. That is the
comparison below; paper_repro/compute_figs.py computes it as
  mavg(choice) = ms_from_p4s(bmm(choice^T, jet_p4))[:, :2].mean(-1)
from the ORIGINAL jet four-momenta, in GeV.

Both models rerun today on the same current samples, full statistics
(11,441 events for the 6j signal), BatchNorm active in both.

  signal                     true    ours <m>_ABC    released <m>_ABC
  gluino_rpv_6j             1500 G   1417 G   -6%   1373 G    -8%
  gluino_rpv_10j            1500 G   1437 G   -4%   1403 G    -6%
  stop_rpv_12j              1500 G   1416 G   -6%   1366 G    -9%
  squark_rpv_8j_WZH_2000    2000 G   1774 G  -11%   1712 G   -14%
  squark_rpv_8j_2000        2000 G   1810 G   -9%   1743 G   -13%
  qcd_background              --      633 G           624 G

  mean |deviation| from the parent mass:  ours 7.2%, released 10.2%

So the mass DOES track the parent, low by 4 to 11%, and our deployed model is
closer than the released checkpoint on every one of the five signals. The
background sits at 633 GeV, well separated from both signal regimes, which is
what makes the mass useful as an observable.

Per-candidate, both models are asymmetric
-----------------------------------------
On gluino_rpv_6j the split is 943/1871 GeV for ours (ratio 1.85) and
1003/1746 GeV for the released checkpoint (ratio 1.74). The asymmetry is a
property of the method, not of our port: the released weights show it too,
and arXiv:2309.05728 ships a brute-force MINIMUM-MASS-ASYMMETRY partition as
a dashed baseline in the same figure, which is the authors measuring exactly
this. That baseline gives 919 GeV on QCD here.

Averaging the two candidates is what makes the observable land near the parent
mass, and it is what the paper reports.

What the released checkpoint actually contains
----------------------------------------------
config_files/2309.05728/finalWeights.ckpt has obj_blocks, cand_blocks and
cross_blocks at indices [0,1,2,3], i.e. FOUR ABC layers, with embed_dim 32,
ae_dim 4 and out_dim (T) = 2, so no ISR slot at all. Our deployed model is
T=3 with two ABC layers and embed_dim 16. The file is named optimal_T2.json,
a tuned variant, so it need not match the architecture described in the paper
text; the block count above is read from the weights, not the config.

Consequences for the earlier mass investigation
-----------------------------------------------
The dead ISR slot (0.00 jets of 9.07) is not a regression against the released
checkpoint: that model is T=2 and has no ISR slot to fill. Two attempts to
revive ours both failed, and are recorded in figs/isr_slot_sweep.txt: the ISR
energy penalty empties the slot at any scale >= 0.05 and at 0 the model
collapses into it (AUC 0.98 -> 0.58), and removing the hardcoded logit bias
entirely does not fill it either while making the split worse (ratio 2.74).

Can a balance-encouraging objective fix the low candidate masses?  No.
================================================================

Background
----------
figs/candidate_mass_diagnosis.txt showed the low reconstructed masses come
from an unbalanced jet assignment, not from quantisation.  The deployed model
sends about 34% of the jets to the lighter candidate instead of 50%, so that
candidate is built from ~3 jets and the other from ~6.  The resulting masses
are 478 GeV and 2215 GeV instead of the ~1660 GeV a balanced split gives.

This file records an attempt to cure that in training, and why it failed.

Method
------
All runs: 2000 steps, seed 7, QCD background, same data and schedule.  Three
families of balance objective were added to model.py, applied to the T=3 jet
choice tensor:

  soft   l_bal = mean((frac_light - 0.5)^2) * scale
  moe    l_bal = 2 * mean(sum_t f_t * P_t) * scale     (switch-transformer
         load balancing; f = hard fraction, P = mean soft probability)
  ST     do_gumbel = True, i.e. gumbel_softmax(tau, hard=True) replaces
         softmax(x/0.1) in get_jet_choice during training, so the penalty
         sees the same one-hot assignment that inference uses.

The 2000-step runs are undertrained next to the deployed model (AUC ~0.90 vs
0.951), so compare them to the balance0 control, not to the deployed row.

Why the soft penalty fails
--------------------------
It is minimised on a tensor that inference never sees.  Training scores
softmax(x/0.1); inference takes argmax.  Recovering the light fraction from
the logged penalty:

  run         TRAIN (soft)        VAL (hard argmax)
  balance2    0.358 -> 0.444      0.299 -> 0.261
  balance10   0.358 -> 0.446      0.284 -> 0.274

In both runs the training fraction moves toward 0.5 and the evaluation
fraction moves away from it.  The model flattens the soft probabilities
without flipping any argmax, which costs it nothing.  The penalty is not
merely ineffective, it is anti-correlated with the quantity we want.

The moe penalty never optimised at all: 5.086 -> 4.974 against a balanced
minimum of 5.0, i.e. it already reports "balanced" at initialisation.

Why straight-through fails worse
--------------------------------
gumbel_softmax(hard=True) is the textbook fix for that train/eval mismatch,
and it collapses the model completely: every jet goes to one candidate, so
m_light is exactly 0.

The control is what identifies the cause.  gumbel0 has the balance penalty
set to zero and collapses exactly as hard as gumbel50; all three ST runs
agree to three digits.  So the collapse is caused by do_gumbel itself, not
by the balance term.  Gumbel adds noise to the logits before the argmax, and
that noise, not the logits, is what spreads the assignment during training.
The model is free to drive the logits further into collapse while the noisy
assignment still looks balanced.  Take the noise away at evaluation and
nothing is left.  gumbel10 shows this directly: the training fraction stays
near 0.35 while the evaluation fraction falls 0.373 -> 0.012.

This is presumably why do_gumbel is False in every shipped config although
gumbel_softmax_config is present.

An architectural change was considered and rejected
---------------------------------------------------
Taking the final jet choice from the object-attention output rather than the
cross-attention output was the obvious next idea.  The probe in
scripts/diagnose_candidate_mass.py rules it out: of the four candidate
tensors, cross1 (what the model already uses) gives the best light share at
34% and obj1 the worst at 27%.  The swap would make the balance worse.

Conclusion
----------
Eight runs across three objective families do not move the light share out of
the 29-37% band, and the one method that acts on the hard assignment destroys
the model.  The unbalanced split should be reported as a property of the
trained model rather than presented as something we corrected.  It does not
affect the hardware result: the fabric and AIE implementations reproduce the
float model's assignment to 1 flip in 13912 jets, so the masses are a
statement about the network, not about the port to hardware.
